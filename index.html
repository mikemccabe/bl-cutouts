A Million Cutout Engravings

Public-domain book illustrations, masked and searchable by description

20 August 2026

Summary

  • The British Library, with Microsoft, digitised 49,455 books published c. 1510–1900 and released 1,080,814 illustrations cut from their pages into the public domain.
  • The cutting was done by software, so the crops are loose: body text above the picture, a printed caption below, the edge of the facing page down one side.
  • As of August 2026, 1,019,266 of them carry a predicted mask — the outline of the picture within its crop. The engraving lifts off the page; the text stays behind.
  • Total compute to outline the entire collection: $3.24.
  • There are no subject tags. Each image instead has a numeric fingerprint of its content, and typed phrases are matched against those fingerprints.
  • A public search service answers such a query in about half a second and composites cutouts on demand, so nothing needs downloading. The demonstration in section 2 runs against it live.
  • The masks are model predictions and have not been checked by hand; publication dates are unreliable at the edges; the 1890s alone account for a third of the corpus.
  • Announcement of the masking work: https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7496149798677557248/
  • Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/biglam/british-library-book-images
  • Search service: https://huggingface.co/spaces/davanstrien/bl-images-search
  • Mask model: https://huggingface.co/davanstrien/bl-crop-tighten-rfdetrseg-clip10
  • Page text of the same books: https://huggingface.co/datasets/biglam/blbooks-parquet

Access recipes

What is published

  • biglam/british-library-book-images, CC0. Image configs embellishments (416,935), plates (385,231), medium (217,100), covers (61,548). Fields image, date (a string), fname, image_type. Full images total about 621 GB.
  • siglip2_embeddings — one 1152-dimensional vector per image from google/siglip2-so400m-patch16-256, row-aligned with the image configs. It is a dual encoder, so text embeds into the same space and ranking is cosine similarity. About 5 GB.
  • crop_masks — 3,022,916 instances over 1,019,266 images, one row per image, joined on fname: objects (bbox, score, area, rectangularity) and masks_rle, COCO RLE in the full source frame. 3.96 GB. covers is excluded, the model being unable to abstain.
  • Scores were kept down to 0.10 so that consumers choose their own operating point; 59.7% of instances fall below 0.3. Filter at score >= 0.3 for display. 76% of images resolve to exactly one instance.

Vector search over the web, without downloading

Cross-origin requests are reflected for any origin, so a static page can call the service directly, as this one does.

B=https://davanstrien-bl-images-search.hf.space
EID=$(curl -s -X POST "$B/gradio_api/call/search" \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"data":["a platypus","all",0,0,8]}' | jq -r .event_id)
curl -s -N "$B/gradio_api/call/search/$EID"
  • /search — query, collection, year_from, year_to, k
  • /search_reference — adds seed_row and text_weight, blending a phrase with an example image
  • /search_feedback — adds positive_rows and negative_rows
  • Schemas at /gradio_api/info and /openapi.json

Single images and cutouts by URL

https://huggingface.co/buckets/biglam/bl-images/resolve/{thumbs|full}/{split}/{fname}
{space}/cutout?fname={fname}&size={thumb|full}    # masked PNG, composited on demand

Pixels outside the mask come back white rather than transparent. That region is connected to the frame, so a flood fill inward from the border converts exactly it to alpha.

SQL against the parquet, in place

DuckDB reads the published files directly with column pruning, so no image bytes move unless the image column is selected.

SELECT i.fname, i.date, m.objects, m.masks_rle
FROM 'hf://datasets/biglam/british-library-book-images/plates/*.parquet' i
JOIN 'hf://datasets/biglam/british-library-book-images/crop-masks/plates-*.parquet' m
USING (fname)

The leading digits of fname are the British Library system number, which is the record_id of the OCR corpus for the same books. The join is at book level, not page level.

One caution: the cutout endpoint is not concurrency-safe

Sixteen cutout requests issued in parallel returned a mixture of 404 no mask for this image and 502 thumb fetch failed; the same thirty-six URLs fetched one at a time returned thirty-six successes. The 404 comes from a shared database handle used across threads, so a mask that exists is reported missing. Serialise the requests, or run two at a time with exponential backoff, and retry rather than trusting the status.

The illustrations are public domain, with no known copyright restrictions; attribution to the British Library is expected practice rather than a legal condition. Nothing in the collection has been reviewed for offensive content, and colonial-era publishing is heavily represented. Queries and cutouts are fetched by your browser from the search service, which sleeps when idle, so the first search after a quiet period may take a minute.
